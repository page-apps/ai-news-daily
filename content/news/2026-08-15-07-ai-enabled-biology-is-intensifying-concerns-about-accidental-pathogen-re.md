## Summary

Recent work has demonstrated AI-assisted design of synthetic viruses able to infect and kill E. coli, while experts warn that AI may allow less-experienced actors to navigate complex biological experiments. The same tools could also improve biodefence, and substantial laboratory and physical barriers remain.

## Why it matters

The concern is not limited to deliberate bioweapons: wider access to advanced biological design and more laboratories could increase accidental-release risk. AI safety assessments are therefore expanding beyond model misuse towards the interaction between software capability and physical experimentation.
