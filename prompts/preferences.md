# Editorial preferences

Edit this file to shape every new edition. It is deliberately versioned with the site so your preferences are transparent and revisable.

## Editorial focus

Prioritise genuinely new, consequential developments in these areas:

- New model capabilities: frontier and open-weight model releases, meaningful changes in reasoning, multimodality, context, memory, tool use, autonomy, efficiency and reliability, supported by evaluations or credible demonstrations.
- New features and products from major LLM providers: model/API updates, agent platforms, developer tools, enterprise products, distribution changes, pricing and deployment capabilities.
- AI security and cybersecurity: model vulnerabilities, prompt injection, jailbreaks, data and model theft, supply-chain risks, abuse, cyber capabilities, defensive uses and security responses.
- AI-related trends: adoption, usage, investment, infrastructure, labour and organisational change, regulation, public sentiment and other shifts that reveal where the technology is heading.
- AGI: credible progress, research directions, evaluation proposals and serious arguments about capability thresholds or timelines. Treat AGI claims as hypotheses to test, not as established facts.
- Physical AI: robotics, autonomous systems, embodied intelligence, industrial automation, simulation and systems that connect models to the physical world.
- Coding agents: software-engineering agents, coding models, IDE and CLI integrations, benchmarks, reliability, security and changes to developer workflows.
- Software engineering and web development: developer platforms, web frameworks, testing, deployment, observability and the practical effects of AI on how software is built, secured and operated.

Do not force every edition to include every topic. Select the ten strongest developments from the generator's explicit rolling 24-hour window. Verify the source's publication or material-update time; never fill the edition with an older story, a resurfaced report or a previously accumulated concept. Give preference to stories that illuminate a capability change, a product consequence or a durable trend.

## Editorial standards

- Prioritise primary sources, original research, evaluations, filings, product releases, incident reports and policy documents.
- Explain what changed, what evidence supports it, who is affected and what remains uncertain.
- Distinguish demonstrated capability from vendor marketing, roadmap claims and speculation.
- Be concise, analytical and sceptical of hype. Prefer durable significance over attention volume.
- Use Australian English.
